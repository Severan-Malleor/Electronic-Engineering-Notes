# 1.3 Atomic Theory: Electron and Holes
* Electrons - A fundamental, _negatively_ charged subatomic particle that orbits the nucleus of an atom. Because they are not made of smaller components, they are primary building blocks of matter and are responsible for chemical bonding and electricity.
* Holes - The absence of electrons

## 1.3.1 The Bohr Model and The Periodic Table
* The Bohr model of the atom states that an atom is composed of a number of ***neutrons*** (Without charge / _neutral_) and ***protons*** (positively charged +) that create the nucleus, which is surrounded by a cloud of orbiting ***electrons*** (negatively charged -)
* Basically neutrons and protons give atoms their mass, while electrons account for the electronic and chemical properties of atoms

Side Note: Even though a proton has much more mass than an electron the charge of a proton and a electron are the same just different signs. This makes an atom electrically neutral
* **Proton:** $+1.602  * 10^{-19}$ Coulombs (positive charge)
* **Electron:** $-1.602 * 10^{-19}$ Coulombs (negative charge

## 1.3.2 Ions and Isotopes

* Ion - If the number of protons or electrons in a _neutral atom_ changes then the atom becomes charged. In most cases, atoms lose or gain electrons, not protons to become ions.
	* Remove an electron (more protons than electrons) -> Positive Ion (cation)
	* Add an electron (more electrons than protons) -> Negative Ion (anion)
	* Small reminder:
		* Ions are almost always formed by moving elections, not protons
		* Adding / removing protons changes the element itself
		* Example:
			* Hydrogen (1 proton) - 1 proton = No atom at all
* Isotope - If an atom loses a neutron.

## 1.3.3 Conductors, Insulators, and Valence
* Electrical current is the flow of electrons from one point to another
* The interesting thing about most metals like Copper (Cu) is that have unpaired (loosed) electrons in their outermost shell, or what is called the ***valence shell***
	* These loose electrons can very easily shift from one atom to another in the lattice and thus create an electron flow or current.
* Insulators are just the opposite of conductors: The valence electrons are tightly bond and thus electrical current does not flow well at all.
	* In most cases, good conductors are single elements, but insulators are usually composed of molecular compounds of two or more atoms like polymer, or even alloys
* Each electron orbital shell can only hold so many electrons based on the theories of quantum mechanics. 
	* Electrons like to be paired with other electrons
	* Due to the fact that electrons have a spin and a clockwise spinning electron always wants to be paired with a counterclockwise spinning partner
	* Orbital shell names
		* 1s
		* 2s, 2p
		* 3s, 3p, 3d
		* 4s, 4p, 4d, 4f
		* 5s, 5p, 5d, 5f
		* 6s, 6p, 6d
		* 7s, 7p
* Electric fields cause the loose valence electrons in the outer shells of the crystal lattice that makes up the piece of metal to move or shift from one position to another
	* This shifting is electron current
* As an electron moves from one atom to another, it leaves a "hole" momentarily. Hence, if we apply an electric field, that is a voltage, this voltage causes electron (negative charges) to move in one direction and holes (positive charges) to move in the other.
	* Holes do not really exist, holes are just a lack of electrons
	* The electron flow is called _electron current_
	* The hole flow is called _conventional current_
* Silicon Doping - The intentional introduction of specific chemical impurities (dopants) into a pure, crystalline silicon lattice to precisely engineer its electrical conductivity
	* Chemical examples are Boron, and Phosphorous
	* Two types of doping
		* N-Type (Negative) - Pure silicon is injected with Group 15 elements like phosphorous or arsenic. Because these elements have _5 valence electrons_, four form bonds with the silicon lattice with the fifth electron is left completely free. These extra unbounded electrons act as negative charge carriers, drastically increasing electrical conductivity
		* P-Type (Positive) - Pure silicon is injected with Group 13 elements, most commonly is _boron_. These elements have only three valence electrons. When they integrate into the silicon lattice, they leave behind an unfilled vacancy known as a "hole". These holes act as a positive charge carriers, drawing in electrons from neighboring atom and allowing electrical current to flow.
