# 1.4 Current and Voltage

## Current Defined
* Current - The amount of charge _q_ (electrons) that passes point _p_ as a function of time f(t)
	* Or mathematically, current is equal to the amount of charge per unite time:
		* Measuring Current = $x = P, \Delta T$ 
		* Current (I) = $\Delta Q / \Delta T$
			* The notation DQ ($\Delta Q$) and DT ($\Delta T$) , simply meaning ***the change in q and the change in t***; this _dq_ / _dt_ is ***The change in q with respect to t***
## 1.4.2 Voltage Uncovered
* Voltage is really a manifestation of the ***electrical field***
* When you place a number of charges together they create an electric field that is the _sum_ of the individual electric fields
	* Given that a charge _q0_ is positioned in an electric field _E_ and we want to move it from point _a to b_, we are going to change the charge's ***potential energy*** by this motion
		* Potential energy is referred to as ***U*** 
* Voltage is the potential energy per unit charge or mathematically:
	* $V = U/q0$ - Definition of voltage. Therefore, the _difference_ in voltage from one point to another is:
		* $V = \Delta V = \Delta U / q0$
* Since potential energy is a form of energy it is measured in Joules (J), and the charge q0 is of course represented by ***coulombs***; therefore, 1 volt is defined as
	* $1V = 1J / 1C$

---
* The potential difference is **always** defined between **two points**. Wherever there is resistance _and_ current flowing, there will be a voltage drop (potential energy being converted to other forms).
* In a simple battery + wire circuit (no other components), the voltage drop happens mostly across the internal resistance of the battery and the small resistance of the wire — which is why shorting a battery can make it get hot or even dangerous.
