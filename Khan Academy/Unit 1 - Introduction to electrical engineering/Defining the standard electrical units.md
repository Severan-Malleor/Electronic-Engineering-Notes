# Ampere
Comes from the study of magnetism.
* Electric currents in wires give rise to magnetic fields
* Those magnetic fields in turn give rise to magnetic forces on the wires
* Two parallel wires carrying current exert a force on each other

# Coulomb
The coulomb is the SI unit of charge
* The size of a coulomb is derived from the ampere.
* One coulomb is defined as the amount of charge flowing when the current is _1 ampere_

1 ampere = 1 coulomb/second

or equivalently

1 coulomb = 1 ampere * second

# Electron Charge
The charge on an electron can be expressed in coulombs as:
# $e = -1.602176565 \times 10^{-19} \,\text{coulomb}$

If we invert this expression, we see that the coulomb can be states in terms of number of electron charges:

# $1 \,\text{coulomb} = 6.241509343 \times 10^{18} \,\text{electrons}$

# Watt
The Watt is the unit of power.
* Power is the amount of energy transferred or consumed per unit of time; equivalently, power is the rate of doing work.
* In standard-speak, the watt is the power which in one second gives rise of 1 joule

# $1 \,\text{watt} = 1 \,\text{joule/second}$

# Volt
The volt is the unit of electric potential difference-electric potential difference is also known as voltage
* The size of 1 volt is officially defined as the potential difference between two points of a wire carrying a current of 1 amp when the power is dissipated in the wire is 1 watt
# $1 \,\text{volt} = 1 \,\text{watt/ampere}$

The volt can also be expressed in terms of energy and charge as,
# $1 \,\text{volt} = 1 \,\text{joule/coulomb}$

# Ohm
The ohm is the electrical unit of resistance
* One ohm is defined as the resistance between two points of a conductor when 1 volt is applied and a current of 1 amp is flowing
# $1 \,\text{ohm} = 1 \,\text{volt/ampere}$

# System of units

There are three main systems of scientific units:
* SI (**S**ystème **I**nternational d'Unités)
* MKS (metre-kilogram-second system)
* cgs

**SI** is the modern form of the metric system and is the most widely used system of measurement
* It is based on the MKS
* In the United States, the SI is used in science, medicine, government, technology, and engineering

**MKS** is based on measuring length in meters, mass in kilograms, and time in seconds
* MKS is generally used in engineering and beginning physics
* The most familiar units of electricity and magnetism - ohm, farad, coulomb, etc - are MKS units

**cgs** is based on measuring lengths in centimeters, mass in grams, and time in seconds
* The cgs system is commonly used in theoretical physics

### SI base units
| Name     | Symbol | Quantity            |
| -------- | ------ | ------------------- |
| meter    | m      | length              |
| kilogram | kg     | mass                |
| second   | s      | time                |
| ampere   | A      | electric current    |
| kelvin   | K      | temperature         |
| candela  | cd     | luminous intensity  |
| mole     | mol    | amount of substance |
One SI base unit comes from electricity: the ampere.
# SI derived units used in electricity
The remaining electrical units are SI derived units, formed by combinations of the base units

| Name    | Symbol   | Quantity                               | In terms of other SI units |
| ------- | -------- | -------------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| coulomb | C        | charge                                 | $A \times s$               |
| watt    | W        | power                                  | $J/s$                      |
| volt    | V        | voltage (electric potential difference | $W/A$                      |
| ohm     | $\Omega$ | resistance, impedance                  | $V/A$                      |
| farad   | F        | capacitance                            | $C/V$                      |
| henry   | H        | inductance                             | $Wb/A$                     |
| hertz   | Hz       | frequency                              | $s^{-1}$                   |
| siemens | S        | conductance                            | $A/V$ or $1/\Omega$        |
| weber   | Wb       | magnetic flux                          | $V \times s$               |
| tesla   | T        | magnetic field strength                | $Wb/m^2$                   |
